Global Semiconductor Shortage Finally Easing as New Factories Come Online

David Park March 21, 2026 6 min read

The global semiconductor shortage that has disrupted industries from automotive manufacturing to consumer electronics for the better part of five years is finally showing signs of meaningful improvement, according to industry analysts and company executives who spoke at the annual Global Chip Summit in Taipei this week.

New fabrication facilities, commonly known as fabs, that were announced during the height of the shortage are now coming online at an accelerating pace. Major chipmakers including TSMC, Samsung, and Intel have collectively invested over three hundred billion dollars in new manufacturing capacity since 2021, and those investments are beginning to bear fruit.

Production Capacity Expanding

TSMC's new facility in Arizona began volume production last month, adding significant capacity for advanced five-nanometer and three-nanometer chips that power everything from smartphones to data center processors. The plant, which represents the largest foreign investment in American semiconductor manufacturing history, is expected to produce over six hundred thousand wafers per year at full capacity.

Meanwhile, Samsung's new fab in Taylor, Texas, has ramped up production of memory chips and logic processors. Intel's facilities in Ohio and Germany are also on track for production starts later this year, further expanding the global supply of cutting-edge semiconductors.

Impact on Industries

The automotive industry, which was among the hardest hit by the shortage, is seeing the most immediate benefits. Car manufacturers had been forced to reduce production, eliminate features, and accept lengthy delivery delays. Now, lead times for automotive chips have dropped from over fifty weeks at the peak of the shortage to approximately twelve weeks, approaching pre-pandemic norms.

Consumer electronics manufacturers are also benefiting from improved supply conditions. The average selling price of memory modules has fallen eighteen percent from its peak, and graphics card availability has improved dramatically. Gamers and professionals who had faced inflated prices and limited stock for years are now finding products readily available at or near manufacturers' suggested retail prices.

Lingering Challenges

Despite the improvements, industry leaders caution that the semiconductor supply chain remains vulnerable. Geopolitical tensions, particularly around Taiwan, continue to cast a shadow over long-term supply security. Additionally, the rapid growth of artificial intelligence applications is creating new demand pressures for specialized chips that could lead to localized shortages in specific product categories.

Leading semiconductor industry analyst Ming-Chi Kuo noted that while overall chip supply has improved substantially, demand for AI accelerators and high-bandwidth memory is growing faster than production capacity can expand. He projected that AI-related chips could face supply constraints through at least 2027 as manufacturers work to bring dedicated AI chip fabrication lines to full capacity.

Government Policies

Government subsidies and industrial policies have played a crucial role in the capacity expansion. The CHIPS and Science Act in the United States, along with similar programs in the European Union, Japan, and South Korea, have provided tens of billions of dollars in incentives to encourage domestic semiconductor manufacturing.

These policies reflect a growing recognition among world governments that semiconductor manufacturing capability is a matter of national security and economic competitiveness. The concentration of advanced chip production in a small number of geographic locations has been identified as a strategic vulnerability that nations are now actively working to address through diversification of their supply chains.

Industry executives at the summit expressed cautious optimism that the worst of the shortage is over, while acknowledging that maintaining a balanced and resilient semiconductor supply chain will require sustained investment and international cooperation for years to come.
